How it works

Three steps, no account

Your goals and plates are kept in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere.

I

Set a goal

Lean out, maintain or build muscle, or type in your own calories and macros. Each meal gets a share of the day.

II

Look at the menu

Every dish on the line today, by station, with a full nutrition label, allergens and ingredients.

III

Tap once

The optimizer picks dishes and portions that land on your targets. Or plan all three meals of the day together.

What you get

The parts that matter

No. 1 · Live from Nutrislice

Nutrition labels for every dish

Calories, protein, carbs, fat, fiber, sodium, sugar and vitamins, per serving.

No. 2 · One tap

An optimal plate

Half-serving precision. Coming up short on protein counts against a plate more than going over, sodium is kept in check, and you can require a vegetable.

No. 3 · All meals

A whole-day plan

Breakfast, lunch and dinner planned together so the day adds up. Shuffle for other combinations.

No. 4 · Saved in your browser

Favorites and filters

Heart the dishes you like, hide allergens, filter vegetarian or vegan. Plates you build are kept so you can come back to them.

Questions

Things people ask

Where does the data come from?
From Nutrislice, the menu system Milton's dining service publishes to. Nothing is typed in by hand, so when the dining hall changes a dish, the app changes with it.
Are the photos the actual dishes?
No. The dining hall doesn't publish photos, so each dish is shown with a stock photo of the same kind of food. The numbers are the real ones.
How accurate is the nutrition info?
It's exactly what the dining service publishes, which is usually right and occasionally odd (a sauce listing every allergen, say). Treat allergen info as a starting point and check with dining staff.
What does "optimal" mean?
Closest to your calorie and macro targets for that meal, with a few rules: being short on protein counts against a plate more than going over, sodium is kept in check, and fiber earns a small bonus.
